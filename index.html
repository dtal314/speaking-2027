The recommended positioning

History explains the change. Real work shows the way.

Use AI history to explain the change, then show people how to apply AI to work they recognize. Our strongest proposals connect a short historical frame to a real case, an audience exercise and a next step participants can use.

How we present and follow through

Use a consistent core story, then change the case, vocabulary and exercise for each audience.

Make the two-presenter format useful

Suggested division: Daniel leads the history and business framing. Jenn or another co-presenter leads the worked example and audience exercise. Match those roles to actual expertise.

Where the call favors end users, include a client or internal process owner who can explain the problem, constraints and observed results.

Prepare one strong submission kit

Speaker evidence. A concise bio, headshot, selected prior talks and a two- to three-minute video showing clear delivery.

Session evidence. A short abstract, three learning objectives, an audience exercise and one usable handout. Tailor the problem to the event.

Case evidence. A before-and-after workflow, actual timing and quality observations, human review effort and a candid explanation of limitations. Describe prototypes accurately.

Delivery readiness. An offline version of the example, a plan for audience participation and agreed roles for every presenter.

Create a clear next step for interested organizations

Share a useful handout through an approved channel. In voluntary follow-up conversations, ask which process is creating friction, who owns it and what improvement would matter.

Suggest a defined next engagement: an executive briefing, workflow assessment, small pilot or staff training program. Match the scope to the problem.

Keep the session educational. Several calls prohibit commercial pitches. AIIM prohibits product demonstrations and does not provide attendee contacts; PSMJ restricts unauthorized solicitation in show areas. Use approved channels and voluntary follow-up. Speaking can build relationships; it does not guarantee clients.

Before committing

Confirm acceptance, session times, registration benefits, travel obligations and event terms. Resolve the ASAE/IAITAM and AIR/PSMJ overlaps. Dates, calls and links were reviewed September 29, 2026 and may change.
